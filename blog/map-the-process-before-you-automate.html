Map the process before you automate it

By Oscar Espinoza, Founder, Alvento | Published 4 October 2026 | 5 min read

Automating a process you have not mapped just makes the mess run faster. Spend an hour drawing the steps first, mark where work is re-typed or stuck waiting, and only then decide what to automate. Here is the four-question method we use.

A pattern we keep seeing

An email comes in. Someone copies it into a spreadsheet. Someone chases it. Someone re-keys it into another system. Someone approves it. Someone files it.

That is six steps and often four people, and nobody can say where a given item is right now. Most of the steps are not hard. They are just manual, and each handover is a place for delay or error.

Step 1: draw what happens today

On one page, list every step from the moment work arrives to the moment it is filed. Write what actually happens, not what the process document says. Include who does each step and which tool they use.

Step 2: ask four questions of every step

  1. Where does the work start? Is it an email, a form, a phone call? Fix the entry point first.
  2. Where is it re-typed? Every place data is copied by hand is a candidate to remove.
  3. Where does it wait for a person? Waiting is where time disappears. Note who it waits for and why.
  4. Is this a rule or a judgment? If the answer is always the same given the same inputs, it is a rule. If someone has to weigh it up, it is judgment.

Step 3: sort the steps

Kind of stepWhat to do with it
Re-typing and copyingAutomate first
Chasing and remindersPut on a fixed schedule
Rule-based checks and matchingAutomate, and log the result
Approvals and exceptionsKeep with a person, but give them a clear summary

Step 4: automate the rules, keep the judgment

The aim is not fewer people. It is fewer things people have to remember or re-type. The judgment calls stay with you, and they get better because you are no longer buried in the rest.

A worked example

Take invoicing. Today: an invoice goes out, someone watches a spreadsheet, someone chases, someone checks the bank, someone updates the sheet. Mapped, the rule-based parts are clear: the reminder schedule, the payment matching and the status update. The judgment part is what to do about a dispute or a long-standing client. That is the split we built into our Arrears reference build, which runs on mock data and is not client work.

Where to start

Pick one process that annoys you. Map it in an hour. If you would like a second pair of eyes on it, process mapping is a good first piece of work with us.
