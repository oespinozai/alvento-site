NullKey vs built-in private folders

NullKey is an Android app that keeps private notes, documents and photos in an encrypted vault on the phone, with its own passphrase and recovery key. Some phones and file managers include a private folder of their own. This page compares the two, and it describes the type of feature in general, not any one phone or app.

The description of built-in private folders is general, because features vary between apps. NullKey facts were checked on 10 November 2026 against the sources below. Spot something out of date? Email hello@alvento.uk.

The honest case for built-in private folders

A built-in folder is the simpler route for a reader who wants to keep a few files out of the gallery and does not want another app. It is already part of the phone or file manager, so there is nothing extra to find, set up or keep updated, and it may use the phone's own screen lock.

It can also be the better fit for a reader who wants the phone maker to handle the details, including how the folder is unlocked and how it connects to the account on the phone. NullKey is a separate app that you set up and manage yourself.

One app everywhere or a feature tied to the phone

A built-in folder belongs to a particular phone or file-manager app. What it offers, and how it behaves, depends on the phone model and the maker's software.

NullKey is one app from Google Play for supported phones running Android 12L or later. Optional biometric and hardware-backed unlock depend on the device.

Unlocking and getting back in

Many built-in folders use the phone's screen lock or a code of their own, and some tie recovery to the phone maker's account. The exact options vary by phone and app.

NullKey uses a passphrase that you choose and a recovery key that you save yourself, with optional biometric unlock, phone screen-lock unlock and an in-app pattern on supported devices. There is no account reset.

Backups, encryption and what you can store

Whether a built-in folder can be backed up or moved, and how its contents are protected, varies by phone and app, so read the maker's own description.

NullKey encrypts each item with AES-256-GCM, keeps notes and documents as well as photos, and makes a whole-vault encrypted backup with a read-back check that you can save wherever you choose.

Bottom line

Choose a built-in private folder if you want a quick separation of a few files with nothing extra to add, and read the maker's description of how it protects them. Choose NullKey if you are on Android 12L or later and want a vault with its own passphrase, a recovery key you keep yourself, no account and an encrypted backup you control.

Frequently asked questions

Is NullKey the same as the private folder on my phone?

No. A built-in private folder belongs to your phone or file manager. NullKey is a separate app from Google Play with its own passphrase, recovery key and encrypted backup.

How do I get back into NullKey if I forget my passphrase?

Use the recovery key that you saved. There is no account reset, and nobody at Alvento can open the vault for you.

Does NullKey need an account or show ads?

No account and no ads.
