NullKey vs Gallery Vault-Hide Photo Video

NullKey is an Android app that keeps private notes, documents and photos in an encrypted vault on the phone, with no sync. Gallery Vault-Hide Photo Video is an Android app from Google Play, and the main differences on this page are disguise features, backup and recovery.

The honest case for Gallery Vault

Gallery Vault-Hide Photo Video is the better fit for a reader who wants the app itself hidden. Its Google Play listing says it supports hiding the app icon, and that it shows substitute content when a separate decoy passcode is entered. The listing also lists fingerprint support, and says the app is for pictures, videos, audio, documents and so on, and that pictures, videos, documents and other types of files can be opened from inside it. Its What's new section names Private Notes. NullKey opens photos and notes inside the app; other files open through a readable export outside the vault's protection.

It is also the closer fit for a reader who wants to reset a forgotten passcode by email, which its Google Play listing describes. NullKey has no account reset; its way back in is a recovery key you save yourself.

Hiding the app and showing substitute content

Gallery Vault's Google Play listing says it supports hiding its app icon, and shows substitute content when a separate decoy passcode is entered. NullKey has no disguise or decoy feature, so its icon is not hidden and no alternate content is shown.

Where files and backups go

Gallery Vault's listing FAQ says hidden files are not stored online and are stored only on the device. Its vendor site lists a Backup and Restore feature for your files. Its listing also says files are decrypted when exported outside the app.

NullKey has no sync and its app declares no internet permission. Its whole-vault backup is encrypted and read back for a check, and you save it wherever you choose through Android's file picker. A readable export of a single item sits outside the vault's protection, in the same way.

Getting back in after a forgotten passcode

Gallery Vault's Google Play listing says a forgotten passcode is reset by following the steps in an email the vendor sends, and that a Forgot button appears after 2 failed unlock attempts. Its listing also states fingerprint and pattern unlock support.

NullKey has no account reset. Its recovery key is one you save yourself, and it opens an existing vault. Optional strong-biometric unlock, phone screen-lock unlock and an in-app pattern are available on supported devices.

Bottom line

Choose Gallery Vault-Hide Photo Video if you want the app icon hidden, a separate decoy passcode that shows substitute content, or a passcode reset by email. Choose NullKey if you are on Android 12L or later and want no ads, no sync, a recovery key you keep yourself and an encrypted vault that declares no internet permission.

Frequently asked questions

Does Gallery Vault show ads or offer in-app purchases?

Its Google Play listing carries the labels Contains ads and In-app purchases. NullKey shows no ads, and its one optional unlock is a one-time purchase with no subscription.

Can either app keep a copy of your files online?

Gallery Vault's listing FAQ says hidden files are not stored online and are stored only on the device, while its vendor site lists a Backup and Restore feature. NullKey has no sync and declares no internet permission, and you save its encrypted backup wherever you choose with Android's file picker.

Does each app encrypt the files it holds?

Gallery Vault's listing says its hidden files are all encrypted. NullKey encrypts its contents with AES-256-GCM, gives each item its own random key, and uses Argon2id to derive a key from your passphrase.
