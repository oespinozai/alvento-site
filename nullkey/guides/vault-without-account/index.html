Practical privacy guide

What does “no account” mean for a private vault?

Less setup, fewer server dependencies, and more responsibility for your own recovery.

Start with what the app can do

An account-free vault can create and unlock encrypted storage without an email address or a remote login. That says nothing by itself about analytics, backups or other network activity. Check those separately.

NullKey’s current boundary

The Android test build has no account, no server and no INTERNET permission. Its encrypted vault lives in the app’s local storage. Android’s document picker can still let you choose an external provider for a backup, and that provider may use the network.

You own the recovery plan

There is no “email me a reset link”. Keep a recovery key for forgotten-passphrase access and encrypted backups for device loss. Losing both unlocking secrets makes existing encrypted data inaccessible; losing the data itself cannot be fixed with a key.

Check the website separately

This product website has no signup form, upload or added analytics scripts. Its hosting infrastructure may process request and operational information. The app privacy policy is published at alvento.uk/nullkey/privacy/; the app’s lack of internet permission does not describe how a browser or website works.
