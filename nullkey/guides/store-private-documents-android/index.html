Practical privacy guide

Where should private documents live on Android?

A practical home for passport scans, certificates and the files you want to keep to yourself.

Start with the copies you already have

A document can exist in your gallery, Downloads, a messaging app and a cloud backup at the same time. Moving one copy does not necessarily remove the others. Before choosing a vault, identify where the document came from and which apps may have copied or backed it up.

Choose the protection you actually need

A hidden album changes visibility. A separate locked folder adds an access boundary. An encrypted vault protects stored contents with encryption and a secret. These protections depend on the implementation and the state of your device. None makes an unlocked, compromised phone trustworthy.

Keep recovery and backup separate

A recovery key helps unlock an existing vault. A backup preserves a copy of the data. To recover after a lost phone, you need a usable backup and the passphrase or recovery key that opens that backup. Store these separately from the phone.

A sensible routine

Start with sample files. Import copies, check their contents and make an encrypted backup. Restore into a separate, empty vault before removing any old app or original. NullKey stores photos, files and notes; PDFs and videos need a readable export to view in another app, outside the vault’s protection.
