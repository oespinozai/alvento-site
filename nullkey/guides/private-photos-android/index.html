How to keep private photos on Android

A practical way to organise private photos, understand Android’s locked folders and keep a recoverable copy.

Decide what “private” needs to mean

A hidden album keeps photos out of a usual gallery view, but may not encrypt the files. A locked folder adds an access check; an encrypted vault protects stored contents using encryption and a secret. Check the exact behaviour on your Android version and phone.

Check the copies you already have

Before importing, look for copies in your camera roll, messaging apps, downloads and any photo sync service you use. Moving one copy does not remove the others. Keep the originals while you test.

Import and check with sample photos first

Install NullKey from Google Play, create a vault and use Android’s picker to import a few sample photos. Unlock the vault and inspect the photos at full size. Importing keeps originals by default; do not enable removal until you understand which local copies can be affected. The private photo vault feature explains what stays protected. Before removing any original, follow the copy-check steps; to compare locked-folder options, read the Android alternatives guide.

Make a backup you can restore

Create a whole-vault encrypted backup and wait for NullKey’s automatic read-back check to confirm it was saved and checked. Keep the backup away from the phone and its matching passphrase or recovery key separately. Practise restoring sample photos into an empty test vault before entrusting it with anything irreplaceable.

Keep expectations realistic

A vault cannot protect a photo while it is visible on an unlocked or compromised device, remove every copy made by another app, or guarantee secure erasure. Cloud providers and Android features have their own storage and recovery rules.
