01, The problem

A missed call is a lead that called someone else.

Home services, dental practices and clinics live or die on the phone, and most of them have a receptionist for exactly the hours they can afford to staff one. Outside those hours, or during a rush, calls go to voicemail, and the caller usually hangs up and rings the next name on the list rather than waiting for a callback.

Missed-call research consistently finds the same pattern: most callers who don't reach a person don't leave a voicemail, and most of those never call back. The exact figure varies by study, but the direction doesn't: every unanswered call is closer to a lost customer than a delayed one.

The fix isn't a script that pretends to be human. It's a system that answers immediately, handles what it can safely handle, and hands off cleanly the moment a call needs real judgement.

04, The approach

Seven decisions that keep it trustworthy on a live line.

A voice agent fails the moment it either sounds like a robot reading a script, or pretends competence it doesn't have. These decisions are what keep it useful instead of embarrassing.

Decision 01

Answer first, qualify second

Every call is picked up immediately, no hold music, no queue. The first exchange establishes what the caller needs before anything else happens.

Decision 02

Booking is a live function call, not a promise

Availability is checked against the real calendar during the call itself, and the booking is confirmed on the spot. Nothing is promised that a follow-up email later has to walk back.

Decision 03

Knows when to hand off

Anything outside a defined scope, a complaint, an emergency, a negotiation, is routed to a person rather than answered badly. The system is built to recognise the edge of its own competence.

Decision 04

Every call logged, nothing lost

Transcript and outcome are recorded automatically for every call, so a missed detail is a two-minute review, not a mystery.

Decision 05

A changed mind doesn't break the flow

"Actually, can we do Tuesday instead?" is handled as a normal turn in the conversation, not a script restart. The call keeps its shape even when the caller doesn't.

Decision 06

Every call becomes CRM data, automatically

In a live deployment, caller details, the booking, and the outcome sync straight into the practice's existing calendar and CRM, whichever ones they already use. No manual re-entry, no second system to keep in step.

Decision 07

A confirmation, not just a call

In a live deployment, appointment confirmations can go by email or SMS once the booking succeeds. The practice gets a separate call summary and access to the transcript, so the team knows what happened and what needs attention.

05, The hand-off

What it sounds like when the call isn't a booking.

A booking call only proves the easy path. This is what happens when a caller needs something outside Foyer's scope, a complaint, not an appointment. Same illustrative-sample caveat applies: synthetic voice, not a real customer.

Caller
I'm unhappy about my appointment last Tuesday. I want to speak to someone in charge.
Foyer

Caller
Michael Doyle. My number is zero seven seven zero zero, nine zero zero, one.
Foyer

Caller
Sorry. Zero seven seven zero zero, nine zero zero, one two four.
Foyer
That's zero seven seven zero zero; nine zero zero; one two four. Is that correct?
Caller
Yes. I just want a proper explanation of what happened.

Foyer
Thank you, Michael. Take care.
