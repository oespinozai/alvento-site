01, The problem

Chasing payment is a job someone already has, done from memory.

Almost every B2B service business runs on invoices: agencies, contractors, wholesalers. The chasing itself is rarely automated. Someone glances at a spreadsheet, remembers who's overdue, and sends a follow-up when they get to it, or when cash flow forces the question. Follow-ups get inconsistent, some clients get chased hard and others get forgotten entirely, and nobody can say with confidence what's actually outstanding right now.

The fix isn't complicated. It's making the cadence structural instead of memorised, so the business doesn't lose money to a task nobody has time to do reliably.

02, The approach

Four decisions that make the system trustworthy.

The value isn't the email-sending. It's the schema and the reconciliation behind it, decided up front so nothing depends on someone remembering to check.

Decision 01

Escalation as data, not memory

Day count since due date drives which template fires: 7, 14, 21, 28, 35, 42. A Python workflow applies the same rules on each scan. Each invoice gets at most one captured reminder per stage; a missed scan catches up at the current stage.

Decision 02

Payment status that updates itself

The payment handler verifies a Stripe-format signature, then matches the invoice reference, customer, GBP amount and currency before marking it paid. The demo uses signed test fixtures; no money moves.

Decision 03

Polite by design, firmer by schedule

Tone escalates with day count, a gentle check-in at day 7, a direct request by day 28, without turning adversarial before it needs to. The words are decided once, not improvised under pressure.

Decision 04

One ledger, one source of truth

SQLite stores invoices, payment events and the captured email outbox. Refresh the page and your demo session keeps its state. Paid invoices are excluded from subsequent reminder runs.

Technical stack

A small stack, with the workflow in view.

Python and Flask run the workflow. SQLite stores the ledger, captured emails and payment events. A systemd timer scans active demo ledgers daily; the buttons run the same cadence logic on demand. The Stripe Python SDK verifies webhook signatures before reconciliation.

Questions

Invoice follow-up, answered.

How does automated invoice follow-up work?

A scheduled job reads the invoice ledger, works out how many days each unpaid invoice is overdue, and sends the reminder for that stage. In Arrears the job runs daily, records every message it generates, and skips any invoice already marked paid, so nobody has to remember who to chase.

When should overdue invoice reminders be sent?

Arrears uses six stages: 7, 14, 21, 28, 35 and 42 days after the due date. Tone starts as a gentle check-in at day 7, asks for a payment date by day 21, and makes a direct request at day 28. Each invoice gets at most one reminder per stage.

How does a payment stop the reminders?

The payment handler verifies the webhook signature, then matches the invoice reference, customer, amount and currency before marking the invoice paid. Paid invoices drop out of every later reminder run, so a client who has paid is never chased again. A payment that matches no single invoice is held for a person to review.

What happens if the same payment event arrives twice?

The ledger records each payment event once. A repeated event is recognised as already processed and ignored, so a webhook retry cannot mark a second payment or change the ledger again.

Is Arrears client work?

No. Arrears is a private, Alvento-owned reference build. The public demo uses fictional invoices, captured emails that are never sent, and simulated payments. A live rollout needs a payment account and an email delivery integration.
